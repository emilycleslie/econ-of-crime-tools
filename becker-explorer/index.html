1 · The offender's gambleAfter Becker I

A person offends when the gamble beats the sure outside option: with probability p they are caught and keep Y − f; otherwise they keep Y. The gain from the offense is fixed at Y = $1,000, as in class.

3 · The planner's mapAfter Becker II

Now you are the planner, and this is your whole problem on one map. You choose only two things: p and f. Your goal is to minimize social loss, L = D(O) + C(p,O) + bpfO, and offenses respond to your choices. Everything else — the cost of punishment b, the elasticity with respect to certainty ηO,p, and the elasticity with respect to severity ηO,f — is a parameter the planner cannot control: together they define the world you are choosing in.

What am I looking at?
Predict before you drag

(1) With b = 0 (fines as pure transfers), where on the map should the valley sit? (2) When detection gets cheaper, which way does the valley move? (3) In the severity-responsive world, what does the planner do with f? Check all three below.

Each dot is the loss-minimizing (p*, f*) at one level of detection cost. The pattern to notice: harsh punishment is what expensive detection looks like. When certainty is cheap, the planner substitutes toward it and severity falls. (Where the path pins against the top edge, the model is at the severity ceiling — the corner from class.) Forward link: DNA databases, when we get to Doleac.

The other two dimensions of the same sweep. Loss at the optimum L* always falls: cheaper detection can only make the planner's problem easier. Offenses at the optimum O* can go either way: more certainty pushes crime down, but milder punishment pushes it up, and which wins depends on the world's parameters.

What this page is and is not. The logic is Becker's; the specific functional forms are illustrative so the sliders have something to move. Parts 1–2: U(c) = cγ; the 100 simulated people have outside options spread evenly from $700 to $997 and γ ranges set by the population switch. Parts 3–4: O(p,f) = 100·(p/0.3)−ηO,p·(f/300)−ηO,f, D(O) = 5 + 0.03·(O − Oa)² with Oa = 40, and C(p,O) = c·p²·O, with c between 4 and 0.5 (the Part-3 checkbox uses 4 and 1.5, so the sweep starts exactly at Part 3's baseline world). Loss is in index units, not dollars. None of these numbers is estimated from data; the shapes, not the levels, are the lesson.
